# PART A — ESG & SUSTAINABILITY

## A1. GRESB — the investor ESG benchmark

GRESB (Global Real Estate Sustainability Benchmark) is the dominant investor-grade ESG benchmark for real assets. The 2025 Real Estate Assessment drew **1,002 fund managers** submitting **2,382 assessments**; 2024 covered **2,223 real-estate participants** representing **~USD 9 trillion** in gross asset value — the scale that makes GRESB the *de-facto* market language.

**Structure (a world-class MD must be able to populate every component):**
- **Management Component** — entity-level: strategy, leadership, policies, processes, risk management, stakeholder engagement.
- **Performance Component** — portfolio operational data: energy, GHG emissions, water, waste (asset-level, year-on-year).
- **Development Component** — sustainability in design, construction, major renovation.
- **Residential Component** — new (inaugural 2025), for portfolios >75% residential by GAV.

**Scoring & rating mechanics:**
- Validation is a three-layer data-quality control with third-party assurance; scoring is automated/objective; results are peer-benchmarked by country, region, sector, investment type.
- The **GRESB Rating** is *relative*: quintile-based stars — top 20% = **5★**, bottom 20% = **1★**; the model is recalibrated annually.
- 2025 benchmarks: Standing Investments average **79** (+3.1 YoY), Development **87.9**, first-time participants **68**. Energy data coverage >75% (2nd year running); **81.5%** of entities now hold net-zero policies. Significant methodology changes go to public consultation in **Q1 2026**.

**Why world-class:** GRESB is how institutional capital *scores* a portfolio's ESG. An autonomous MD that can self-assess against the four components, predict the star rating, and identify the marginal points (waste-data coverage, net-zero policy, asset-level energy intensity) is operating at allocator standard.

## A2. Green-building certifications — LEED, BREEAM, EDGE (and the East-Africa choice)

**LEED (USGBC).** Points-based: **Platinum 80+, Gold 60–79**, Silver, Certified. Active in 185 countries, 200,000+ projects. **LEED v5 launched 28 April 2025** — a major restructuring around three impact areas: **Decarbonization 50% / Quality of Life 25% / Ecological Conservation & Restoration 25%**, with *mandatory* carbon assessment, *mandatory* embodied-carbon LCA, and all-electric credits now central.

**BREEAM (BRE).** Broader scope than LEED — energy *plus* health, pollution, management, ecology. Dominant in Europe (~80% share); **BREEAM In-Use** is the operational/refurb scheme (UK registrations +340% 2023→2025), the right tool for *existing* stock.

**EDGE (IFC / GBCI)** — **the most Borjie-relevant.** Built *for emerging markets*; certifies new and existing buildings achieving **≥20% cuts in energy, water, AND embodied energy in materials** vs a conventional baseline. EDGE is the *only* major system that makes embodied-energy efficiency a certification parameter. Levels: **EDGE Certified (20/20/20)**, **EDGE Advanced (≥40% energy)**, **EDGE Zero Carbon (100% operational carbon neutrality via renewables/offsets)**. Free design software + streamlined online certification + large certifier network → faster/cheaper than LEED/BREEAM. IFC projects USD 24 trillion of green-building investment in emerging-market cities this decade.

**MD decision rule:** for Tanzania/Kenya/Uganda/Nigeria mine-estate buildings (staff housing, offices, processing-plant admin blocks), **EDGE is the default** — emerging-market calibration, embodied-energy credit, IFC-finance alignment. Reserve LEED/BREEAM for trophy/holdings assets in markets where investors demand them.
*Source: https://edge.gbci.org/ · https://www.ifc.org/en/what-we-do/sector-expertise/climate-business/promoting-sustainable-innovation/green-buildings · https://blog.corecastre.com/corecast-blog/leed-vs-breeam-key-differences · https://www.levelworkspace.com/post/green-building-certifications-uk-guide*

## A3. EU Taxonomy + SFDR + CSRD — the disclosure triangle

The frameworks interlock: **EU Taxonomy** defines *what is a sustainable activity*; **SFDR** mandates *financial-product disclosure against it*; **CSRD** mandates *corporate sustainability reporting*. Real estate matters because **buildings are ~40% of EU GHG emissions**.

- **EU Taxonomy & real estate.** Real-estate activity is taxonomy-eligible; technical screening criteria define **seven financing categories** — new construction, renovation, acquisition/ownership, installation of equipment, technological upgrades, etc. An activity must *substantially contribute* to ≥1 of six environmental objectives, **do no significant harm (DNSH)** to the others, and meet **minimum (social) safeguards**.
- **SFDR classifications.** **Article 6** (no sustainability focus), **Article 8** ("light green" — promotes E/S characteristics), **Article 9** ("dark green" — sustainable investment as objective). Article 8/9 funds promoting *environmental* characteristics must make EU-Taxonomy alignment disclosures; an Article 8 fund promoting only *social* characteristics need not disclose against the environmental objectives.
- **Anti-greenwashing** is the spine of the whole regime.

**Why world-class:** an MD raising EU or DFI capital (IFC, EIB, development banks active in East Africa) must classify products correctly and produce Taxonomy/DNSH/safeguards evidence. Misclassification = greenwashing risk + capital lock-out.
*Source: https://finance.ec.europa.eu/sustainable-finance/tools-and-standards/eu-taxonomy-sustainable-activities_en · https://www.gresb.com/understanding-the-relationship-between-eu-taxonomy-sfdr-and-csrd-and-their-impact-on-real-estate/ · https://blog.worldfavor.com/sfdr-what-is-article-6-8-9 · https://www.catalyst-group.com/thinking/understanding-the-relationship-between-eu-taxonomy-sfdr-and-csrd-and-their-impact-on-real-estate/*

## A4. TCFD → ISSB (IFRS S1 / S2) — climate disclosure for real assets

**The transition is now done:** the TCFD has been folded into the ISSB's IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards. **IFRS S2 Climate-related Disclosures** (issued June 2023, effective for annual periods from **1 Jan 2024**) *integrates and builds on* TCFD and pulls in **SASB** industry metrics (incl. the Real Estate industry).

**The four pillars (identical TCFD architecture):**
1. **Governance** — board/management processes, controls, procedures to oversee climate risk/opportunity.
2. **Strategy** — climate risks/opportunities, their financial effects, and **climate resilience via scenario analysis**.
3. **Risk Management** — identify, assess, prioritise, monitor.
4. **Metrics & Targets** — performance, targets, **Scope 1/2/3 GHG**.

**What IFRS S2 adds beyond TCFD** (the world-class delta): (a) **industry-specific metrics** from SASB across 11 sectors / 70+ industries; (b) **quantified anticipated financial effects** of material climate risks across short/medium/long horizons; (c) a globally consistent classification. Both **physical** (acute/chronic hazards — flood, heat, water stress on a mine camp) and **transition** risks (carbon pricing, MEES-type regulation, stranding) must be disclosed where they could affect cash flows, financing access, or cost of capital. As of mid-2025, **20+ jurisdictions (>50% of global GDP)** have adopted or are adopting ISSB. (ISSB issued GHG-disclosure relief amendments in Dec 2025 to ease implementation.)

**Why world-class:** real-estate funds with significant assets *will* need to disclose physical climate risk under IFRS S2 or CSRD. An MD that runs scenario analysis on a portfolio (flood/heat exposure of mine housing; carbon-price sensitivity of plant) and reports it in the S2 four-pillar structure is operating at listed-issuer standard.
*Source: https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/ifrs-sustainability-standards-navigator/ifrs-s2-climate-related-disclosures/ · https://www.mitigasolutions.com/blog/ifrs-s1-and-ifrs-s2 · https://continuuiti.com/blog/tcfd-vs-issb/ · https://verdani.com/sustainability-industry-insights/transitioning-from-the-task-force-on-climate-related-financial-disclosures-tcfd-to-the-ifrs-sustainability-disclosure-standards*

## A5. Embodied + operational carbon, net-zero pathways, whole-life carbon

**RICS Whole Life Carbon Assessment (WLCA), 2nd edition — in full effect since 1 July 2024.** The world reference for measuring a built asset's full life-cycle carbon. Uniquely, it gives visibility to **embodied carbon + operational carbon + user carbon**. RICS members *must* follow it when doing WLCAs.

- **Operational carbon** — emissions from running the building (energy use); *reversible* via retrofit/renewables.
- **Embodied carbon** — emissions locked into materials/construction/end-of-life; **irreversible once released** — the carbon you can never get back, so it must be designed out up-front.
- **User carbon** — RICS-distinctive third category (occupant-driven emissions).
- **Net-zero anchoring** — the **UK Net Zero Carbon Building Standard (NZCBS)** sets science-based targets/limits for an asset to be *recognised* as net-zero, with measurement *per RICS WLCA*.

**Why world-class:** "net zero" is meaningless without WLCA boundaries (life-cycle modules A–C/D) and a credible pathway. An MD that can compute embodied vs operational carbon and judge a retrofit's carbon payback (CRREM analysis of 36 global retrofits found payback up to ~8 years) reasons like a chartered sustainability engineer.
*Source: https://www.rics.org/profession-standards/rics-standards-and-guidance/sector-standards/construction-standards/whole-life-carbon-assessment · https://www.rics.org/profession-standards/rics-standards-and-guidance/sector-standards/construction-standards/whole-life-carbon-assessment/unlocking-sustainability-exploring-rics-whole-life-carbon-assessment-wlca-standard · https://www.rics.org/news-insights/wbef/the-uk-s-whole-life-carbon-ecosystem-a-framework-for-decarbonising-the-built-environment*

## A6. CRREM — stranding risk and decarbonisation pathways

**CRREM (Carbon Risk Real Estate Monitor)** is the global standard for *transition risk* in real estate. Born under EU Horizon 2020, now run by the independent **CRREM Foundation**. It **downscales** the Paris-aligned global GHG budget → country → real-estate sector → property type → individual asset, producing an **intensity pathway** (kgCO₂e/m²/yr and kWh/m²/yr).

- Pathways are aligned to **2°C with ambition toward 1.5°C**.
- An asset whose carbon/energy intensity rises *above* its pathway line becomes a **stranded asset**.
- The **CRREM Misalignment Year** = the first year an asset's performance exceeds its allocated 1.5°C budget — the single most decision-relevant number for capex timing.

**Why world-class:** CRREM turns abstract climate targets into a per-asset stranding date. An MD that overlays each estate building's measured intensity on its CRREM pathway, flags the misalignment year, and sequences retrofit capex to avoid stranding is doing exactly what leading institutional asset managers do.
*Source: https://www.crrem.eu/global-decarbonisation-pathways/ · https://www.crrem.eu/objectives-and-benefits/ · https://crrem.org/ · https://www.crrem.eu/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/CRREM-Risk-Assessment-Reference-Guide-V2_20_03_2023.pdf*

## A7. Energy performance — EPC & MEES (the regulatory carbon stick)

**EPC** = the asset's energy rating (A–G). **MEES (Minimum Energy Efficiency Standard)** makes low ratings *unlettable*.

- **Now (England & Wales):** privately-rented commercial property must be **≥ EPC E** to be lawfully let (some exemptions).
- **Trajectory:** evidence points to commercial MEES rising to **EPC B after 2030 (before 2035)**, with a likely **interim EPC C by ~2027**. A large share of stock is below B — e.g. ~**58% of Central London office floorspace** is sub-B.
- **Penalties:** up to **£150,000** depending on breach duration and property value.

**Why world-class (and the transferable lesson for East Africa):** MEES is the template for how energy regulation *strands lettable value*. East-African jurisdictions will follow (building codes, green-finance conditions). An MD must treat EPC/energy rating as a *value and lettability* input, not a compliance afterthought, and model the capex to clear the next rung.
*Source: https://www.cbre.co.uk/insights/articles/what-can-we-expect-from-the-upcoming-changes-to-minimum-energy-efficiency-standards · https://www.rinnai-uk.co.uk/rinnai-knowledge-hub/rinnai-news/minimum-energy-efficiency-standards-mees-what-epc-b-means-uk-commercial-property-2030 · https://energyadvicehub.org/whats-happening-with-the-non-domestic-minimum-energy-efficiency-standards/*

## A8. Social value & affordable housing (the "S")

**Why world-class:** the "S" is the least standardised and most reputationally explosive ESG pillar in extractive industries. # PART B — FORMAL VALUATION

## B1. The governing standards: RICS Red Book Global + IVS

**Two interlocking authorities** define world-class valuation:
- **IVS (International Valuation Standards Council)** — the global baseline. **New edition published 31 Jan 2024, effective 31 Jan 2025.**
- **RICS Valuation – Global Standards ("Red Book Global")** — published **December 2024, effective 31 January 2025**, and it **incorporates the full IVS text in Part 6**, cross-referencing each VPS to IVS. RICS is mandatory for its members worldwide.

**RICS Red Book 2025 structure** (the MD must know the skeleton):
- **PS 1 / PS 2 — Professional Standards** (mandatory; "Must" language). PS 2 carries the global governance requirements (competence, ethics, independence) that apply even where regulated-purpose rules don't.
- **VPS 1–6 — Valuation Technical & Performance Standards** (renumbered in 2025):
  - **VPS 1** Terms of engagement (lists **18 mandatory items** incl. valuer/client identity, purpose, basis of value, valuation date; must now state any significant **ESG** considerations).
  - **VPS 2** Bases of value, assumptions & special assumptions (transaction costs now handled here).
  - **VPS 3** Valuation approaches and methods.
  - **VPS 4** Inspections, investigations and records.
  - **VPS 5** Valuation models.
  - **VPS 6** Valuation reports.
- **VPGAs — Valuation Practice Guidance Applications** — purpose-specific: notably **VPGA 1** financial reporting, **VPGA 2** secured lending (see B6).
- New 2025 content addresses **modelling, automation, AI, and AVMs** (automated valuation models) per the Valuation Review.

**Why world-class:** these are *the* standards used for bank lending, fund NAVs, IPO prospectuses, and litigation globally. Producing a Red Book/IVS-compliant scope and report is the dividing line between an opinion and a defensible valuation.
*Source: https://www.rics.org/profession-standards/rics-standards-and-guidance/sector-standards/valuation-standards/red-book/red-book-global · https://www.rics.org/content/dam/ricsglobal/documents/standards/Red-Book-Global-Standards-incorporating-IVS.pdf · https://www.property-elite.co.uk/post/red-book-2025 · https://ivsc.org/new-edition-of-the-international-valuation-standards-ivs-published/*

## B2. IVS 2025 restructure (the modern valuation process)

The IVS General Standards were **reordered to mirror the actual valuation workflow**, with **three brand-new chapters**:
- **IVS 100 Valuation Framework** → **IVS 101 Scope of Work** → **IVS 102 Bases of Value** → **IVS 104 Data & Inputs (NEW)** → **IVS 105 Valuation Models** → **IVS 106 Documentation & Reporting (revamped)**.
- **IVS 103 Valuation Approaches** (Market / Income / Cost).
- **IVS 500 Financial Instruments (NEW asset standard).**
- **Expanded ESG consideration**, including a **dedicated appendix**.

The two new infrastructure chapters — **Data & Inputs** and **Documentation** — codify the world-class disciplines of *data-quality/selection* and *auditable record-keeping*; the ESG appendix makes sustainability a first-class valuation input.
*Source: https://ivsc.org/new-edition-of-the-international-valuation-standards-ivs-published/ · https://acumentis.com.au/news/new-international-valuation-standards-ivs-effective-january-2025/*

## B3. Bases of value (the question "value to whom, on what assumption?")

An MD must select the **basis** *before* the method — it is the single most common error to conflate them. IVS-defined bases:

| Basis | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Market Value** | The estimated amount an asset would exchange for in an **arm's-length** transaction between knowledgeable, willing parties after proper marketing — the default for most real-property valuations. |
| **Market Rent** | The lease counterpart of Market Value. |
| **Investment Value / Worth** | Value **to a specific owner/investor** given their objectives (subjective; the input to buy/hold/sell decisions). |
| **Equitable Value** | The price **fair to two identified parties**, reflecting their respective advantages/disadvantages — *not* general market value. |
| **Synergistic Value** | Extra value from **combining** assets/interests (marriage value). |
| **Liquidation Value** | Value in a **forced sale or orderly liquidation** scenario. |

Plus, for regulatory/accounting purposes, **Fair Value (IFRS 13 / IAS)** — distinct from IVS Equitable Value despite the name overlap.

**Why world-class:** lenders want **Market Value** (occasionally Mortgage Lending Value); accounts want **Fair Value**; an investor's bid is driven by **Investment Value/Worth**; a partition dispute needs **Equitable Value**. Choosing the wrong basis voids the report's fitness for purpose.
*Source: https://saicawebprstorage.blob.core.windows.net/uploads/resources/IVS-effective-31-January-2025.pdf · https://www.studocu.vn/vn/document/hoc-vien-chinh-sach-va-phat-trien/thi-truong-chung-khoan/international-valuation-standards-ivs-effective-31-jan-2025/154276905 · https://ivsc.org/new-edition-of-the-international-valuation-standards-ivs-published/*

## B4. The three approaches and the five methods

IVS 103 defines **three approaches** (Market, Income, Cost); RICS practice maps them onto **five methods**. They are *not mutually exclusive* — modern practice often cross-checks with more than one before reporting.

**1. Comparable / Sales-Comparison method — Market approach.**
Derives value from analysed transactions of similar properties (adjusted for differences). The primary method wherever a deep, evidenced market exists (standard residential, commercial land, generic offices/retail). The evidential backbone for nearly every other method too.

**2. Investment method — Income approach (traditional / cap-rate).**
Capitalises income into capital value. Core mechanics: **Value = NOI ÷ Cap Rate**; for reversionary interests, **term & reversion** (capitalise current rent to lease event, then capitalise reversionary market rent). Yield vocabulary the MD must distinguish: **all-risks yield, initial / net initial yield, equivalent yield, reversionary yield, exit/terminal cap rate**. Suits tenanted, income-producing assets.

**3. Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) — Income approach (explicit).**
Projects an **explicit period of cash flows + a reversion (exit value)** and discounts at a **target rate / opportunity cost of capital**. The reversion is computed by direct capitalisation: **Reversion = (NOI in year after holding period) ÷ Exit Cap Rate**. Critical sensitivity: the reversion can exceed *half* of total PV in a 10-yr DCF, and a **50 bps move in the exit cap rate can shift value 5–10%**. Suits complex/irregular income, development, value-add, and any ESG-retrofit cash-flow modelling. RICS publishes dedicated DCF practice guidance.

**4. Residual method — Cost/development appraisal.**
For **development sites**: *Gross Development Value − (construction + fees + finance + developer's profit) = Residual land value*. Highly sensitive to inputs (the classic "garbage-in" method); best paired with a sensitivity/scenario analysis.

**5. Profits method — Income approach (trade-related).**
For assets whose value is inseparable from the **business** run from them (hotels, petrol stations, leisure, **mine-camp trade-related facilities, processing operations**): value the **maintainable trading profit / EBITDA / FMOP**, then capitalise.

**6. Depreciated Replacement Cost (DRC) — Cost approach.**
*Cost of a modern-equivalent replacement − depreciation/obsolescence (physical, functional, economic) + land value.* The method of **last resort** for **specialised assets with no market** — exactly the profile of much **mining infrastructure**: processing plants, tailings facilities, purpose-built workshops. RICS has dedicated DRC guidance for financial reporting.

**Why world-class:** matching method to asset type — and never DRC'ing something that has a market, never comparable'ing a specialised plant — is the hallmark of a competent valuer. For Borjie's mixed estate, the MD will routinely run **comparable** (land/housing), **investment/DCF** (let commercial holdings), **profits** (trading/processing operations), and **DRC** (specialised plant) in a single portfolio.
*Source: https://www.isurv.com/info/1024/valuation_approaches · https://ww3.rics.org/uk/en/journals/property-journal/apc-5-valuation-methods.html · https://www.rics.org/content/dam/ricsglobal/documents/standards/Discounted-cash-flow-valuations-1.pdf · https://www.rics.org/content/dam/ricsglobal/documents/standards/drc_method_of_valuation_for_financial_reporting_1st_edition_rics.pdf · https://www.hellodata.ai/help-articles/reversion-value-in-real-estate · https://intval.com/articles/Where-the-Overall-Cap-Rate-Meets-the-Discount-Rate.pdf*

## B5. DCF vs cap-rate — when each, and the link

- **Direct capitalisation (cap rate)** — fast, market-implied, best for **stabilised** income; the cap rate *embeds* growth and risk assumptions implicitly.
- **DCF** — explicit, best when cash flows are **irregular, growing, or being changed** (lease-up, refurb, ESG retrofit, phased development).
- **The link:** in a stabilised, no-growth world the **overall cap rate = discount rate − growth**; the discount rate is the **opportunity cost of capital** (required total return for comparable risk). A world-class MD reconciles the two: the cap-rate cross-check on a DCF, and the implied-DCF behind a cap rate.
*Source: https://intval.com/articles/Where-the-Overall-Cap-Rate-Meets-the-Discount-Rate.pdf · https://www.rics.org/content/dam/ricsglobal/documents/standards/Discounted-cash-flow-valuations-1.pdf · https://ryanoconnellfinance.com/cre-dcf-proforma/*

## B6. When a Red Book valuation is **mandatory for lending** + the conflicts regime

**Secured-lending valuations are governed by RICS VPGA 2** ("valuations for secured lending, including mortgage valuations"), updated and *simplified* in the 2025 Red Book.

- **Mandatory governance (PS 2) always applies** — including for secured lending. *However*, in the **UK National Supplement**, secured lending is **NOT a "regulated-purpose" valuation under VPS 3**, so the mandatory **valuer-rotation** requirement does **not** apply to it (a frequently-missed nuance).
- **Conflicts of interest are the heart of secured lending.** The valuer **must confirm objectivity and independence for every instruction**, check for conflicts **before accepting**, keep a clear record, and obtain the client's **informed consent** to proceed. Any **previous or current involvement** with the property/parties triggers **additional disclosures**.
- **Basis for lending:** typically **Market Value** (with any special assumptions clearly stated); some jurisdictions/banks also use **Mortgage Lending Value (MLV)** — a deliberately sustainable, through-the-cycle value (RICS publishes "Bank lending valuations and mortgage lending value").
- **ESG is now a required consideration** even in lending: VPS 1 terms of engagement must capture significant ESG factors.

**Practical rule for the MD:** *any* time the estate borrows against an asset (working-capital facility, plant finance, holdings-company mortgage), the lender will require an **independent, Red Book/IVS-compliant Market Value** from a valuer with **documented no-conflict** — produced to VPGA 2 with VPS 1 terms of engagement.
*Source: https://ww3.rics.org/uk/en/journals/property-journal/getting-secured-lending-valuations-right.html · https://ww3.rics.org/uk/en/journals/property-journal/global-red-book-updates.html · https://www.rics.org/content/dam/ricsglobal/documents/standards/BANKLE_1.pdf · https://www.rics.org/profession-standards/rics-standards-and-guidance/sector-standards/valuation-standards/red-book/red-book-uk*

## B7. East-Africa valuation practice — who may value, under what law

This is **load-bearing for Borjie** (Tanzania-first jurisdiction). In East Africa, valuation is **statutorily regulated** — only registered valuers may sign valuations, and Red Book/IVS is the professional overlay, not the statute.

**Tanzania (launch jurisdiction).**
- Governing law: the **Valuation and Valuers Registration Act, 2016 (Act No. 7 of 2016)** (consolidated/R.E. 2023), regulating the valuation profession in **Tanzania Mainland**.
- Establishes the **Valuers Registration Board (VRB)** — registers valuers; oversees conduct, ethics, complaints; issues **practising certificates**.
- A **Chief Valuer** (within the Ministry responsible for lands) heads the Valuation Department and advises Government on valuation matters.
- **Registration categories:** fully registered, temporarily registered, provisionally registered; a practising certificate is required to *practise*.
- Historical scope of valuation work: compensation (Land Acquisition Act), rating (Urban Authorities (Rating) Act), mining (Mining Act), tax (Income Tax Act), and professional-surveyors legislation — i.e. **mining and land-compensation valuations are explicitly within the regulated remit**, directly relevant to Borjie.

**Kenya.**
- **Valuers Registration Board (VRB)** — statutory regulator under the **Valuers Act, Cap 532**: registers, licenses, regulates valuers.
- **Institution of Surveyors of Kenya (ISK)** — the *professional body* (Valuation Chapter among others): development, training, advocacy (the regulate-vs-represent split mirrors RICS-vs-statute elsewhere).
- **Path to practise:** degree/diploma in Land Economics / Real Estate / Valuation → **Full Member of ISK (Valuation Chapter)** → **VRB Practising Certificate, renewed annually**.

**Uganda / Nigeria (expansion):** analogous statutory-registration models exist (Uganda: Surveyors Registration Board; Nigeria: ESVARBON / NIESV) — **UNVERIFIED** in this session; confirm before relying.

**Why world-class:** an autonomous MD must know that in Tanzania/Kenya a valuation is only *valid* if signed by a **registered/licensed valuer** under the relevant Act, applying IVS/Red Book methodology — and that **mining and compensation valuations** are squarely inside the regulated regime. It must route formal valuations to a qualified human valuer and structure its own analysis to be *consistent with* that statutory + IVS framework, never as a substitute for the registered signature.
