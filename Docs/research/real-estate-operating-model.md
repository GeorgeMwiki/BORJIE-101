## 0. Executive thesis — what "world-class" means in real estate operations

A world-class real estate operating model is not a collection of departments; it is a **single capital-allocation loop** that runs continuously from deal sourcing through disposition, instrumented end-to-end with data, governed by recognized professional standards (RICS Red Book / IVS, RIBA, ASTM, GRESB), and increasingly orchestrated by agentic AI. The defining shift in 2025–2026 is that AI underwriting and due-diligence have moved **from differentiator to baseline**: Goldman Sachs estimated AI tools could reduce CRE due-diligence costs by 20–35% for large institutional portfolios, and CBRE's 2025 Tech Adoption work found teams using AI for underwriting completing preliminary analysis ~3× faster. The autonomous MD's job is to run that loop with discipline, evidence, and standards-compliance at every node.
Source: https://thefractionalanalyst.com/tfa-blog/ai-in-commercial-real-estate-finance ; https://collateral.com/blog/how-ai-is-reshaping-real-estate-underwriting-and-due-diligence

The loop has **eleven primary functions**, each detailed below:

1. Acquisition & deal sourcing
2. Due diligence (title / zoning / environmental / survey / financial)
3. Development & project delivery
4. Leasing & tenancy management
5. Rent, arrears & collections
6. Facilities / property management & PropTech
7. Portfolio & asset management
8. REIT / fund operations & capital stack
9. Valuation & appraisal (RICS Red Book / IVS)
10. Brokerage & marketing
11. Ancillary income
…wrapped by **ESG/sustainability** as a cross-cutting governance layer, and grounded in **East-Africa land tenure & regulation**.

## 1. Acquisition & deal sourcing

### World-class practice
The acquisitions function is the top of the capital-allocation funnel. World-class firms separate **origination** (filling the pipeline) from **execution** (closing). Sourcing channels: broker/owner/developer relationships, direct outreach to asset owners, conference networks, and JV partnerships with local/regional operators who control proprietary or lightly-intermediated deal flow. The strategic edge is targeting situations where **fragmentation and complexity limit the buyer universe** — i.e. proprietary deal flow rather than fully-marketed auctions.
Source: https://agorareal.com/blog/sourcing-and-underwriting-real-estate-deals/ ; https://www.thesisdriven.com/letters/deep-dive-alpaca-real-estate/

A senior acquisitions VP "quarterbacks the entire acquisition lifecycle, from pitch to purchase" — shaping the initial investment thesis and risk/return framework, overseeing underwriting, and navigating the **investment committee (IC)**.
Source: https://umbrex.com/resources/career-paths-in-real-estate/acquisitions-investment-analysis-careers/

### State-of-the-art pipeline & underwriting
- Dedicated deal-pipeline software (e.g. Dealpath, Altrio) is now table stakes for institutional acquisitions and pipeline management.
  Source: https://www.dealpath.com/acquisitions/ ; https://altrio.com/
- **Agentic AI** coordinates whole sequences across the deal lifecycle — pulling market data, running comps, building the proforma, stress-testing the model, flagging risks, **drafting the IC memo**, and routing exceptions to human reviewers. Fundraising cycles lengthened to ~24 months by 2025, pressuring teams to screen more deals without proportional headcount growth — exactly the leverage agentic AI provides.
  Source: https://thefractionalanalyst.com/tfa-blog/ai-in-commercial-real-estate-finance
- **DSCR (debt-service coverage ratio) stress-testing** with a multi-method framework is a hallmark of institutional underwriting rigor; banks using AI underwriting report 50–75% reductions in time-to-decision.
  Source: https://www.mmcginvest.com/post/dscr-under-stress-a-three-method-framework-for-institutional-underwriting

### Autonomous-MD capabilities
- Maintain a scored, stage-gated **deal pipeline** with origination-channel attribution.
- Auto-generate an **underwriting proforma** (DCF + return metrics) and a structured **IC memo** with thesis, risk/return, sensitivities, and exception flags.
- Run **DSCR and downside stress scenarios** before any IC decision; never let a deal pass IC without a documented stress case.

## 2. Due diligence (title / zoning / environmental / survey / financial)

### World-class practice
CRE due diligence spans four categories — **financial** (income verification, rent rolls), **physical** (building condition, environmental), **legal** (title, zoning, leases), and **operational** (management, contracts) — and typically runs **30–90 days**.
Source: https://www.businessscreen.com/resources/commercial-real-estate-due-diligence-guide-checklist ; https://realestatedatarooms.com/blog/commercial-real-estate-due-diligence-checklist-2026-documents-timeline-data-room/

### The standards-grade checklist
- **Title & survey:** Obtain the title commitment, **ALTA/NSPS Land Title Survey**, and legal description. The ALTA survey confirms boundaries, easements, encroachments, setbacks, and flood-zone status, and carries certifications required by title companies and lenders.
  Source: https://propertymetrics.com/real-estate-due-diligence-checklist/ ; https://raphaelcollazo.com/commercial-property-due-diligence-checklist/
- **Zoning:** Request a **zoning verification letter** from the local planning authority confirming the property's zoning permits the intended use.
  Source: https://www.clearhouselending.com/blog/commercial-due-diligence-checklist-guide
- **Environmental:** A **Phase I Environmental Site Assessment (ESA)** following **ASTM E1527-21** is required by virtually every commercial lender and provides CERCLA/Superfund liability protection. Phase I identifies **Recognized Environmental Conditions (RECs)** (contamination, asbestos, hazardous materials); if RECs are found, a **Phase II ESA** with soil/groundwater sampling follows.
  Source: https://aeiconsultants.com/phase-i-environmental-site-assessment-checklist/ ; https://www.epa.gov/land-revitalization/revitalization-ready-guide-chapter-3-reuse-assessment

### Autonomous-MD capabilities
- Drive a **data-room-backed DD workflow** with a 30–90-day clock, document-completeness tracking, and a four-category (financial/physical/legal/operational) rubric.
- Trigger and track **ALTA survey, title commitment, zoning verification letter, and Phase I ESA (ASTM E1527-21)** as gating artifacts; auto-escalate to Phase II on any REC.
- Surface **encroachment / easement / flood-zone / setback** exceptions as red flags against the proforma.

## 3. Development & project delivery

### World-class practice — RIBA Plan of Work 2020
The canonical framework for briefing, designing, constructing and operating buildings is the **RIBA Plan of Work 2020**, organizing projects into **eight stages (0–7)**:
- **Stage 0** Strategic Definition (may end in the decision to initiate)
- **Stage 1** Preparation & Briefing
- **Stage 2** Concept Design
- **Stage 3** Spatial Coordination
- **Stage 4** Technical Design
- **Stage 5** Manufacturing & Construction
- **Stage 6** Handover
- **Stage 7** Use (ongoing in-use evaluation; loops back to a new Stage 0 on repurpose/refurbish)

Each stage defines **stage outcomes, core tasks, and information exchanges**. The 2020 edition better accommodates **design-and-build procurement, off-site manufacturing, and digital coordination**, and weights **sustainability from Stage 0 through in-use evaluation at Stage 7**.
Source: https://www.riba.org/work/insights-and-resources/riba-plan-of-work/ ; https://www.riba.org/media/syneeeto/2020ribaplanofworkoverviewpdf.pdf ; https://architectureforlondon.com/news/the-riba-plan-of-work/

### Development feasibility & viability
Development viability is assessed with the **residual valuation method** (Gross Development Value minus total development cost minus profit yields the residual land value) — this is the development-specific cousin of the income approach (see §9).
Source: https://www.jpmorgan.com/insights/real-estate/commercial-real-estate/commercial-real-estate-valuation-approaches

### Autonomous-MD capabilities
- Run development projects on **RIBA Stages 0–7** with stage-gate outcomes, core tasks, and information exchanges tracked per stage.
- Compute and re-run **residual land-value / GDV feasibility** at Stage 0–1 and refresh at each design milestone.
- Embed **sustainability targets from Stage 0** (embodied + operational carbon) and carry them to in-use evaluation at Stage 7 (ties to GRESB, §ESG).

## 4. Leasing & tenancy management

### World-class practice — the lease lifecycle
Lease management provides strategic oversight across the **lease lifecycle**: setting acceptable terms, managing **critical dates**, and aligning portfolio decisions with investment goals; tenant management drives renewals through screening, onboarding, communication, maintenance SLAs, and community-building.
Source: https://www.re-leased.com/lease-tenant-management/ultimate-guide-to-lease-tenant-management-2026 ; https://www.occupier.com/blog/the-lease-life-cycle/

### Core institutional metrics
- **WALT (Weighted Average Lease Term):** the rental-income-weighted average remaining lease term across all tenants; a higher WALT signals more stable near-term income and lower short-term leasing risk.
  Source: https://www.adventuresincre.com/glossary/weighted-average-lease-term/
- **Net Effective Rent (NER):** average rent per period after **concessions** (TI allowances, graduated rent, rent-free fit-out periods, opex caps) are amortized across the lease term — the truth-telling rent figure vs. face/headline rent.
  Source: https://www.re-leased.com/terms/net-effective-rent

### Tenant retention
Retention is the **most efficient path to NOI growth** — replacing a tenant typically costs more than targeted retention investment. Best practice: criteria-based screening, structured onboarding (condition reports, policy orientation, portal setup, emergency contacts), and proactive renewal management.
Source: https://www.steadily.com/blog/tenant-retention-strategies-for-long-term-success ; https://www.daviscountypropertymanagementinc.com/blog/lease-renewal-strategies-how-to-retain-valuable-commercial-tenants

### Autonomous-MD capabilities
- Maintain a **rent roll + lease abstract** with critical-date alerting (breaks, expiries, rent reviews, options).
- Compute **WALT** and **NER** (face vs. effective) at asset and portfolio level; flag concession creep.
- Run a **renewal/retention engine** that surfaces at-risk tenants ahead of expiry and quantifies retention-vs-replacement cost.

## 7. Portfolio & asset management

### World-class practice — the two distinct disciplines
- **Asset managers** are like "division presidents" — execute strategies to **optimize NOI, increase property value**, and ensure each asset contributes to portfolio objectives (the business-plan executors at asset level).
- **Portfolio managers** oversee groupings (by geography, asset class, fund, or mandate), ensuring alignment with **capital-allocation goals, risk tolerance, and return targets** — the strategic link between ownership objectives and real-time asset performance.
- Strategy cascades **down** from PM to AM; operational insight bubbles **up** from AM to inform PM strategy.
Source: https://umbrex.com/resources/career-paths-in-real-estate/asset-portfolio-management-careers/

### Hold/Sell discipline
**Hold/Sell analysis** determines whether to keep an income asset or sell and reinvest. Professionals run it **regularly** using a **DCF** to compare the hold-scenario return against the sell-and-reinvest return, optimizing portfolio returns vs. a target benchmark. Modern practice uses ML to flag underperformers and recommend optimal hold/sell timing.
Source: https://www.adventuresincre.com/glossary/hold-sell-analysis/ ; https://www.growthfactor.ai/blog-posts/portfolio-management-real-estate

### Autonomous-MD capabilities
- Maintain **asset-level business plans** with NOI-growth levers and value-add tracking (AM layer).
- Run **portfolio construction** by geography/class/mandate with risk-tolerance and return-target alignment (PM layer).
- Run **recurring hold/sell DCF analysis** and benchmark portfolio returns; recommend rotation/reinvestment.

## 8. REIT / fund operations & capital stack

### World-class practice — the capital stack
The capital stack layers (senior → junior, lower-risk → higher-risk/higher-return): **senior debt → mezzanine debt → preferred equity → common equity**. Senior positions are protected if a project underperforms; junior positions capture upside if it overperforms.
Source: https://sitgcapital.com/understanding-waterfall-structures-private-equity-real-estate-funds/ ; https://www.northspyre.com/blog/equity-waterfall-real-estate

### The distribution waterfall
Five core components: **return of capital → preferred return → catch-up → promote (carried interest) → return hurdle**. LPs typically receive a **preferred return of 6–10%** (often non-compounded) before the GP participates; sponsors then "catch up" and earn an outsized **promote** on cash flow above target. Hurdles are usually expressed as **IRR**.
Source: https://www.jpmorgan.com/insights/real-estate/investing/equity-waterfall-in-commercial-real-estate-explained ; https://fastercapital.com/content/Preferred-Return--Maximizing-Preferred-Return--A-Guide-to-Distribution-Waterfall-Strategies.html

Worked example (JPMorgan): a $10M multifamily deal, 60% debt / 40% equity, sponsor 25% of equity / investors 75%, three tiers:

| Tier | IRR hurdle | Sponsor share | Investor share |
|------|-----------|--------------|----------------|
| 1 | up to 10% | 25% | 75% |
| 2 | 10–15% | 35% | 65% |
| 3 | 15–20%+ | 50% | 50% |

Source: https://www.jpmorgan.com/insights/real-estate/investing/equity-waterfall-in-commercial-real-estate-explained

**American vs. European waterfalls:** American (deal-by-deal) lets the GP earn carry on single investments before LPs recover all capital; European (whole-fund) returns all cash pro-rata to LPs until they receive preferred return **and** invested capital back, then the GP earns carry. Management fees typically **1–2%** of committed/managed capital.
Source: https://www.wallstreetprep.com/knowledge/real-estate-waterfall/ ; https://www.altusgroup.com/insights/how-are-real-estate-funds-structured/

### Autonomous-MD capabilities
- Model the full **capital stack** (senior/mezz/pref/common) and compute layer-by-layer risk/return.
- Run a **distribution-waterfall engine** (return of capital → pref → catch-up → tiered promote on IRR hurdles), supporting both American and European structures and 1–2% mgmt fee accrual.
- Produce **LP/GP distribution statements** and fund-level NAV/return reporting (and REIT distribution compliance — see §EA REITs).

## 9. Valuation & appraisal (RICS Red Book / IVS)

### World-class practice — the standards stack
- **RICS Valuation – Global Standards ("Red Book")** is the global professional standard; the current edition became **effective 31 January 2025**, aligned to the new **International Valuation Standards (IVS)** (published 31 Jan 2024, effective 31 Jan 2025). The Red Book incorporates IVS and **cross-references each VPS to IVS**.
  Source: https://www.rics.org/profession-standards/rics-standards-and-guidance/sector-standards/valuation-standards/red-book/red-book-global
- **Red Book structure (effective 31 Jan 2025):** mandatory **Professional Standards (PS)** plus **Valuation Technical & Performance Standards (VPS 1–6)**, restructured to mirror the valuation process: **VPS 1** Terms of engagement; **VPS 2** Bases of value, assumptions & special assumptions; **VPS 3** Valuation approaches & methods; **VPS 4** Inspections, investigations & records; **VPS 5** Valuation models; **VPS 6** Valuation reports. PS1 now carries mandatory standards for **AVMs (Automated Valuation Models)** and new content on **automation/AI** and **ESG**.
  Source: https://www.rics.org/profession-standards/rics-standards-and-guidance/sector-standards/valuation-standards/red-book/red-book-global
- **IVS 2025 structure:** 7 General Standards + 8 Asset Standards. General Standards: **IVS 101** Scope of Work, **IVS 102** Bases of Value, **IVS 103** Valuation Approaches, **IVS 104** Data & Inputs, **IVS 105** Valuation Models, **IVS 106** Documentation & Reporting — reordered to mirror the modern valuation process, with new chapters on **Data & Inputs, Documentation, and Financial Instruments**.
  Source: https://ivsc.org/new-edition-of-the-international-valuation-standards-ivs-published/ ; https://saicawebprstorage.blob.core.windows.net/uploads/resources/IVS-effective-31-January-2025.pdf

### The valuation approaches/methods
Three recognized approaches (with real-estate-specific methods):
1. **Income capitalization approach** — value = NOI / cap rate. Two methods: **direct capitalization** (single-year income) and **yield capitalization / DCF** (multi-year cash flows discounted). Primary approach for income-producing assets.
2. **Sales comparison approach** — comparison to recent comparable sales, adjusted for size, location, condition, etc.
3. **Cost approach** — replacement/reproduction cost minus depreciation plus land value.
Plus development-specific **residual method** and trade-related **profits method** for operational assets (hotels, etc.).
Source: https://www.jpmorgan.com/insights/real-estate/commercial-real-estate/commercial-real-estate-valuation-approaches ; https://propertymetrics.com/blog/income-approach/ ; https://www.dealpath.com/blog/income-approach-appraisal-direct-capitalization-method/

### Autonomous-MD capabilities
- Produce **Red Book / IVS-compliant valuations**: define scope (VPS 1 / IVS 101), select **basis of value** (market / fair / investment — VPS 2 / IVS 102), apply **approaches** (income/comparison/cost/residual — VPS 3 / IVS 103), document **data & inputs** (IVS 104), use auditable **valuation models** (VPS 5 / IVS 105), and issue a compliant **report** (VPS 6 / IVS 106).
- Implement **direct cap, DCF, comparison, cost, and residual** methods; treat any AVM output under **PS1 AVM** governance with disclosed assumptions/limitations.
- Carry **ESG factors** into value (mandated by the 2025 Red Book) — e.g. EPC/CRREM stranded-asset risk feeding cap-rate/discount adjustments.

## 13. East-Africa market & regulatory specifics (TZ / KE / UG / NG)

Borjie launches in **Tanzania** and plans **Kenya / Uganda / Nigeria** expansion. Land tenure and REIT regimes differ sharply by jurisdiction — a world-class platform must encode each.

### 13.1 Tanzania (launch jurisdiction)
- **All land is public**; nobody holds soil in perpetuity. Governed by the **Land Act, Cap. 113 [R.E. 2023] (originally Land Act No. 4 of 1999)** and the **Village Land Act 1999**.
- Three land categories: **General Land, Reserved Land, Village Land**.
- Tenure is a **Right of Occupancy** for **33, 66, or up to 99 years** (often "99 years less 10 days"), evidenced by a **Certificate of Title**; village land carries **Customary Rights of Occupancy**.
- **Foreigners cannot own land directly.** A non-citizen obtains a **Derivative Right** — a long-term lease-like interest in General Land tied to an approved investment — via the **Tanzania Investment Centre (TIC)**, which holds the title and grants the derivative right to the investor. Requires a **TIC Certificate of Incentives**, typically with a **~USD 500,000** minimum investment threshold.
- Administered by the **Commissioner for Lands** (President's agent for General Land) and **village authorities** (village land).
Source: https://makafrica.com/guide-for-foreigners-on-land-ownership-under-mainland-tanzania-laws/ ; https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2019/03/land-acquisition-in-tanzania-what-foreign-investor ; https://landportal.org/news/2016/09/tanzania-no-foreigner-can-own-land-tanzania-except-investment-only

### 13.2 Kenya
- **REITs** are governed by the **Capital Markets (Real Estate Investment Trusts) (Collective Investment Scheme) Regulations** (gazetted **28 June 2013**, amended **6 October 2023**), regulated by the **Capital Markets Authority (CMA)** and listed on the **Nairobi Securities Exchange (NSE)**.
- Two types: **D-REITs** (Development REITs — pool capital for development/construction) and **I-REITs** (Income REITs — long-term income-generating real estate).
- Tax: REITs are largely **exempt from income tax** (subject to withholding tax on interest/dividends) and, in cases, from **stamp duty, VAT, and capital gains tax**.
- Affordable-housing push via the **Kenya National REIT (KNR)** (CMA + Sanduku + pension trustees).
Source: https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/ln/2013/116/eng@2023-10-06 ; https://cma.or.ke/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/REITS.pdf ; https://www.nse.co.ke/real-estate-investment-trusts/ ; https://www.tripleoklaw.com/the-tax-regime-for-reits-in-kenya/

### 13.3 Uganda
- **Four land tenure systems:** **Freehold, Mailo, Leasehold, Customary** (Land Act / Constitution).
- **Mailo** is measured in square miles and **splits ownership between registered owner (landlord) and lawful/bona fide occupants (tenants)** — a major DD risk.
- **Leasehold** terms typically **49 or 99 years** (Land Act §3(5)); registered under the **Registration of Titles Act, Cap. 230**. A **non-citizen** can hold a lease of **five years or more**, registered under the RTA.
- Administered by the **Ministry of Lands, Housing & Urban Development** (Department of Land Registration).
Source: https://nalaw.org/2025/02/18/land-ownership-in-uganda-tenure-challenges-and-legal-framework/ ; https://blog.propertystreet.ug/land-tenure-in-uganda-explained-customary-freehold-mailo-and-leasehold/ ; https://mlhud.go.ug/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/Registration-of-Titles-Act-Chapter_230.pdf

### 13.4 Nigeria
- **Land Use Act 1978:** all land in a State is **vested in the Governor**, held in trust for all Nigerians. Tenure is a **Right of Occupancy** evidenced by a **Certificate of Occupancy (C of O)**, typically for **99 years**.
- **Section 22:** any sale, mortgage, or transfer is **unlawful without the Governor's consent** ("Governor's consent first had and obtained") — a critical transaction gate.
- **REITs:** governed by the **Investment and Securities Act (ISA) 2007**, **SEC Consolidated Rules & Regulations (2013)**, **CAMA 2020**, **Finance Act 2021**, and the **Land Use Act 1978**, regulated by the **Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC)** and listed on the **NGX**. A closed-ended REIT must hold **≥75%** of total assets in real estate, **≤25%** in real-estate-related assets, and **≤10%** of the remainder in liquid assets.
Source: https://lawsofnigeria.placng.org/print.php?sn=228 ; https://legaldoc.ng/articles/property-law/governors-consent-under-the-land-use-act-when-is-it-really-required ; https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=97c7cdc6-9875-4756-beae-29ce89bf1514 ; https://www.aluko-oyebode.com/insights/structuring-a-reit-in-nigeria-key-legal-and-tax-considerations/
