## 1. The Voluntary-Standard Spine

### 1.1 ICMM Mining Principles + Performance Expectations (the membership floor)

The International Council on Mining and Metals (ICMM) is the convening body for the world's largest responsible-mining companies (29 company members + 36 associations). Membership is conditional on implementing the **Mining Principles**, which were enhanced and re-issued (**December 2024 update**) to strengthen social and environmental requirements on labour rights, resettlement, gender, grievance access, mine closure, pollution and waste.

**The 10 Mining Principles** (titles, synthesized from ICMM disclosures):
1. **Ethical business** — anti-corruption, transparency, ethical governance.
2. **Decision-making / sustainable development** — integrate sustainable-development considerations into corporate decision-making.
3. **Human rights** — respect human rights and the interests/cultures/rights of employees and affected communities (UNGP-aligned).
4. **Risk management** — implement effective risk-management strategies and systems based on sound science.
5. **Health & safety** — pursue continual improvement and ultimately the elimination of fatalities, injuries and occupational disease.
6. **Environmental performance** — pursue continual improvement, prevention of pollution, responsible production and waste management.
7. **Conservation of biodiversity** — contribute to biodiversity conservation and integrated land-use planning (no-go in World Heritage sites).
8. **Responsible production** — facilitate and support responsible product design, use, re-use, recycling and disposal across the value chain.
9. **Social performance** — pursue continual improvement in social performance and contribute to the social/economic development of host communities.
10. **Stakeholder engagement** — proactively engage stakeholders on sustainable-development challenges; transparent and accountable reporting.

**Performance Expectations (PEs)** sit beneath the 10 Principles as detailed, auditable criteria. Validation framework (member requirements):
- **Self-assessment** of all applicable assets **once every three years**.
- **Third-party validation** of *prioritised* assets within the three-year validation cycle, by qualified **Validation Service Providers (VSPs)** that meet ICMM independence/expertise/no-conflict criteria.
- **Annual disclosure** of validation activity.
- Per-PE outcomes are scored **'Meets' / 'Partially Meets' / 'Does Not Meet' / 'Not Applicable'**.

> **Why world-class:** ICMM membership + validated PE conformance is the de-facto investability floor for major lenders and off-takers. An autonomous MD must maintain a live PE register per asset, schedule the 3-year cycle, and pre-empt 'Partially/Does Not Meet' findings.

Sources: https://www.icmm.com/en-gb/our-principles/mining-principles/mining-principles · https://www.icmm.com/en-gb/members/member-requirements/mining-principles/mining-principles · https://www.teck.com/media/Teck-ICMM-Performance-Expectations-Self-Assessment-and-Validation-Disclosure.pdf · https://www.boliden.com/4952cf/globalassets/about-boliden/new-about-boliden/our-policies-and-commitments/policy-documents/icmm-mining-principles-compliance-2024.pdf

Related ICMM position statements an MD must track: **Climate Change** (net-zero Scope 1+2 by 2050), **Indigenous Peoples and Mining** (Aug 2024, FPIC), **Water Stewardship**, **Tailings (GISTM adoption)**.

### 1.2 IRMA — Initiative for Responsible Mining Assurance (the independent-audit gold standard)

IRMA is the most rigorous *independently-governed, multi-stakeholder, mine-site-level* assurance scheme. Governance gives **equal voice** to six stakeholder sectors (no single-sector capture) — distinguishing it from industry-run schemes like ICMM/TSM. It applies to industrial mining and on-site mineral processing, defining best practice through **400+ auditable requirements** organised under **4 Principles / 26 chapters**.

**4 Principles & key chapters:**
1. **Business Integrity** — legal compliance, community & stakeholder engagement, human-rights due diligence, complaints/grievance mechanisms, revenue/payment transparency.
2. **Planning for Positive Legacies** — ESIA, **FPIC**, community benefits, resettlement, emergency preparedness, reclamation & closure planning.
3. **Social Responsibility** — fair labour & working conditions, occupational H&S, community H&S, mining in conflict-affected areas, security arrangements, ASM interface, cultural heritage.
4. **Environmental Responsibility** — waste/materials management, water management, air quality, noise/vibration, GHG emissions, biodiversity & protected areas, cyanide & mercury management.

**Achievement levels** (the differentiator): **IRMA Transparency** (audit conducted & published) → **IRMA 50** → **IRMA 75** → **IRMA 100**. Levels reflect the percentage of requirements met; **critical requirements** must be met to achieve any non-Transparency level. Audits are **independent third-party** against the published Standard. **IRMA-Ready** is a self-assessment on-ramp.

**v2.0 status:** Standard 2.0 went through public consultation (1st in 2023; 2nd from 22 Jul–24 Oct 2025) — an MD must track the v2.0 finalisation and re-baseline.

> **Why world-class:** IRMA is increasingly demanded by EV/battery, jewellery and electronics off-takers as proof of responsible sourcing at the *mine site*, not just the corporate group. Achieving IRMA 75/100 is a genuine market-access and premium-pricing lever.

Sources: https://responsiblemining.net/what-we-do/standard/irma-mining-standard/ · https://responsiblemining.net/what-we-do/standard/standard-2-0/ · https://responsiblemining.net/what-we-do/standard/ · https://minsus.net/mineria-sustentable/IRMA/IRMA-Standard-Guidance.pdf

### 1.3 GISTM — Global Industry Standard on Tailings Management (the dam-safety law of the industry)

Co-convened by **ICMM + UNEP + PRI** after the Brumadinho disaster. Goal: **zero harm to people and the environment** with **zero tolerance for human fatality**. Structure: **6 Topic Areas / 15 Principles / 77 auditable requirements** across the full TSF lifecycle. Now stewarded by the **Global Tailings Management Institute (GTMI)** for independent certification/auditing.

**The 15 Principles by topic area:**

- **Topic I — Affected Communities:** P1 Zero harm to people & environment; P2 Respect the rights of project-affected people (engagement, grievance); P3 Understand social/environmental/economic context.
- **Topic II — Integrated Knowledge Base:** P4 Develop & maintain an integrated knowledge base; P5 Robust risk-management system (define tolerances).
- **Topic III — Design, Construction, Operation & Monitoring:** P6 Design/construct/operate/monitor for integrity (extreme-event design, real-time monitoring); P7 Closure & post-closure plan from the start (perpetual safety); P8 Best Available Technology / Best Available Practice.
- **Topic IV — Management & Governance:** P9 Responsible corporate culture & governance (board oversight); P10 Assign accountability across the organisation; P11 Independent oversight (EoR + Independent Review Board).
- **Topic V — Emergency Response & Long-term Recovery:** P12 Prepare for emergencies before they happen; P13 Plan for recovery & remediation (pre-arranged funds); P14 Regularly review & test emergency plans.
- **Topic VI — Public Disclosure & Transparency:** P15 Publicly disclose facility-level information.

**Consequence classification** (five classes, based on *consequence of failure* regardless of likelihood): **Low → Significant → High → Very High → Extreme**. A facility carries a *Current Classification* and a *Classification used for Design* (which may be higher — e.g., a High facility designed to Extreme). New TSFs / upgrades default to **extreme design criteria**.

**Mandatory roles:**
- **Accountable Executive** — a single named senior officer accountable for the safety of each facility, reporting to the board.
- **Engineer of Record (EoR)** — required for *every* facility.
- **Responsible Tailings Facility Engineer (RTFE)** — operational engineering responsibility.
- **Independent Tailings Review Board (ITRB)** — required for **Extreme** and **Very High** consequence facilities; **senior independent technical review** for all other classes.

**Implementation timeline:** ICMM members conformed for **Extreme/Very High** facilities by **Aug 2023**; **all other** facilities by **Aug 2025**. Members publish annual progress.

> **Why world-class:** A single TSF failure is an existential, life-and-licence event. GISTM conformance (with named Accountable Executive, EoR/RTFE, ITRB for high-consequence dams, and Principle-15 public disclosure) is now demanded by lenders, insurers and the Church of England Pensions Board / PRI investor coalition.

Sources: https://globaltailingsreview.org/global-industry-standard/ · https://blog.gistm.net/blog/a2-gistm-15-principles-practical-guide/ · https://www.icmm.com/progress-implementing-gistm · https://www.angloamerican.com/esg-policies-and-data/tailings-summary/our-approach-to-gistm

### 1.4 TSM — Towards Sustainable Mining (the association-led, site-level, externally-verified system)

Created by the **Mining Association of Canada (MAC) in 2004**; now adopted by mining associations in **Australia, Finland, Norway, Argentina, Spain, Botswana, Brazil, the Philippines** and others. From **2025, Minerals Council of Australia (MCA)** members must assess & publicly report against TSM indicators. TSM is **facility-level, indicator-graded (Level C → AAA), and externally verified every 3 years** with CEO/board letters of assurance.

**Eight performance protocols:** Tailings Management; Biodiversity Conservation Management; Community Outreach (formerly Indigenous & Community Relationships); Energy & GHG Emissions Management; Crisis Management & Communications Planning; Safety & Health; Water Stewardship; Preventing Child & Forced Labour.

> **Why world-class:** TSM's facility-level letter-graded scorecard (with public, externally-verified results) is the model for *measurable, comparable* site performance. An MD can mirror TSM's protocol/indicator structure internally even where TSM membership is not available.

Sources: https://tsminitiative.com/protocols-frameworks · https://mining.ca/towards-sustainable-mining/ · https://mining.ca/towards-sustainable-mining/protocols-guides/tailings-management-protocol/ · https://mining.ca/towards-sustainable-mining/protocols-guides/biodiversity-conservation-management/

## 2. The Disclosure Spine

### 2.1 ISSB — IFRS S1 + S2 (the consolidated global baseline; absorbs TCFD)

The **International Sustainability Standards Board (ISSB)** issued **IFRS S1** (General Requirements for Disclosure of Sustainability-related Financial Information) and **IFRS S2** (Climate-related Disclosures) in **June 2023**, effective for annual periods beginning **on/after 1 Jan 2024**. **IFRS S2 fully integrates and builds on the TCFD recommendations** and incorporates **industry-based disclosure derived from SASB** — the **TCFD was disbanded in 2023 and its monitoring handed to the ISSB**. This is now the *single global baseline*.

**Four core content areas** (same TCFD architecture):
1. **Governance** — processes, controls & procedures to monitor/manage/oversee climate risks & opportunities.
2. **Strategy** — climate risks & opportunities, their effects on business model/strategy/cash-flows, **scenario analysis** (climate resilience), and **transition plans**.
3. **Risk & opportunity management** — how risks/opportunities are identified, assessed, prioritised, monitored.
4. **Metrics & targets** — cross-industry metric categories + targets.

**GHG-emissions requirements (the bite for mining):**
- Disclose **Scope 1, Scope 2, and Scope 3** GHG emissions.
- **Material Scope 3** must be disclosed **across all 15 GHG-Protocol categories** (broader than TCFD) — for mining, downstream processing/use of sold minerals usually dominates.
- Measured per the **GHG Protocol Corporate Standard**.
- Consolidated groups disaggregate emissions for the group vs other investees.
- Cross-industry metrics: also internal carbon prices, capital deployed, executive-remuneration links to climate.
- **Industry-based metrics:** the SASB-derived Metals & Mining set (see §2.2).

**Sequencing for mining:** A widely-cited transition gives oil/gas/mining/cement/auto/non-renewable-power a phased ramp (e.g., industry-metric relief in early years); **UNVERIFIED** the exact "2027" full-implementation date attributed in secondary sources — confirm against each jurisdiction's adoption instrument. Adoption is jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction (UK, Canada, Australia, Nigeria, etc.); the MD must map which ISSB-aligned local regime binds each entity.

> **Why world-class:** ISSB is becoming the lender/exchange-mandated disclosure language globally. Scope-3 across all 15 categories + scenario analysis + transition plan is the heavy lift an MD must build data pipelines for *now*.

Sources: https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/ifrs-sustainability-standards-navigator/ifrs-s2-climate-related-disclosures/ · https://www.ifrs.org/news-and-events/news/2023/06/issb-issues-ifrs-s1-ifrs-s2/ · https://www.ifrs.org/content/dam/ifrs/supporting-implementation/issb-standards/progress-climate-related-disclosures-2024.pdf · https://www.ey.com/en_us/insights/ifrs/what-you-need-to-know-about-new-issb-standard-ifrs-s2 · https://www.spglobal.com/sustainable1/en/insights/research-reports/june-2025-where-does-the-world-stand-on-issb-adoption

### 2.2 GRI 14: Mining Sector 2024 (impact materiality) + SASB Metals & Mining (financial materiality)

**GRI 14: Mining Sector 2024** is the first GRI sector standard for mining — **published 2024, effective 1 January 2026** (V1.1 Jan 2026 aligned to revised GRI climate/energy topic standards). It identifies the topics **likely to be material** for any mining organisation (commonly cited as ~**24–25 topics**) and points to the specific GRI disclosures for each, used with the GRI Universal & Topic Standards.

**Topics include** (impact lens — impact on people/environment/economy): **tailings management; ASM (artisanal & small-scale mining); operations in conflict-affected areas** (three topics new to GRI and specific to mining); plus climate-change & emissions, biodiversity, water & effluents, waste, occupational H&S, human rights, **land & resource rights**, anti-corruption, **payments to governments**, community engagement, closure & rehabilitation, economic impacts.

**SASB Metals & Mining (EM-MM)** — now under ISSB stewardship and embedded as the IFRS S2 industry-based metrics for mining — covers ~**12 financially-material topics** (GHG emissions, air quality, energy/water/waste management, biodiversity impacts, security/human-rights & rights of indigenous peoples, community relations, labour relations, workforce H&S, business ethics & transparency, tailings storage facilities management, plus activity metrics).

**Materiality distinction the MD must operate:**
- **GRI = impact materiality** — the company's impacts *on* the world (regulator, community, civil-society audience).
- **SASB/ISSB = financial materiality** — sustainability risks/opportunities' impact *on enterprise value* (investor/lender audience).
- A world-class MD reports **double materiality** (both), as EU CSRD requires and as GRI×ISSB interoperability guidance enables.

> **Why world-class:** GRI 14 (effective 2026) sets the *impact* expectation; SASB/ISSB sets the *financial* one. Reporting both, asset-by-asset, is the 2026 standard of care.

Sources: https://www.globalreporting.org/standards/standards-development/sector-standard-for-mining/ · https://www.globalreporting.org/media/n1wjkac1/gri14_implementation_guide_for_reporters.pdf · https://www.globalreporting.org/media/2bocmv0b/240729-accompanying-guide.pdf · https://www.isometrix.com/gri-14-mining-sector-2024-a-new-era-for-mining-sustainability-reporting-in-2026/

### 2.3 EITI — Extractive Industries Transparency Initiative (revenue/contract/ownership transparency)

The **EITI Standard 2023** is the global benchmark for transparency & accountability in oil, gas & mining. The **2023 revision** adds: **energy-transition** disclosures, embeds **anti-corruption** into many requirements, strengthens **beneficial-ownership** and **contract** disclosure, and adds **gender** considerations.

**The (eight) EITI Requirements:**
1. **Oversight by the multi-stakeholder group (MSG)** — government + companies + civil society, with work-planning.
2. **Legal & institutional framework, contracts & licences** — licence-allocation process, public **registers**, **contract disclosure**, **beneficial-ownership** disclosure (legal owners + share, full chain toward beneficial owner), state participation.
3. **Exploration & production** — sector overview, production & export data.
4. **Revenue collection** — disclose **all material company-to-government payments**, reconciled, with disaggregation & data quality.
5. **Revenue management & distribution** — traceability into budgets; subnational transfers.
6. **Social & economic spending** — mandatory social/environmental payments, quasi-fiscal SOE spending, economic contribution.
7. **Outcomes & impact** — accessible disclosure feeding evidence-based public debate.
8. **Compliance / Validation** — periodic independent Validation scoring implementation.

> **Why world-class:** EITI alignment (especially beneficial-ownership + contract + payment-to-government disclosure) is the antidote to the "resource curse" narrative and a precondition for development-finance and many off-take relationships. See §4.7 for Tanzania's TEITI implementation.

Sources: https://eiti.org/eiti-requirements · https://eiti.org/eiti-standard · https://eiti.org/sites/default/files/2023-06/2023%20EITI%20Standard.pdf

### 2.4 TNFD — Taskforce on Nature-related Financial Disclosures (the "TCFD for nature")

TNFD published **final recommendations in September 2023**: a risk-management & disclosure framework for **nature-related dependencies, impacts, risks and opportunities (DIROs)**. **14 recommended disclosures** across the **same four pillars** as TCFD/ISSB (**Governance, Strategy, Risk & Impact Management, Metrics & Targets**) — deliberately interoperable with ISSB and GRI.

**The LEAP approach** (assessment methodology):
- **L — Locate** your interface with nature (spatial data: where in the value chain you touch sensitive/priority ecosystems).
- **E — Evaluate** dependencies & impacts on nature.
- **A — Assess** material nature-related risks & opportunities.
- **P — Prepare** to respond and report.

**Metrics:** because nature has no single "GHG-equivalent" metric, TNFD provides **14 core global metrics** (drivers of nature change: land/freshwater/sea-use change, pollution, resource use, invasive species, plus state-of-nature) **+ sector-specific metrics**, including a **Mining & Metals** sector guidance (TNFD published draft sector guidance for four sectors incl. mining/metals).

> **Why world-class:** With the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework (Target 15) pushing mandatory nature disclosure, TNFD is the emerging investor expectation. Mining's footprint on land-use change, water and protected areas makes LEAP + mining sector metrics directly applicable.

Sources: https://tnfd.global/publication/additional-guidance-on-assessment-of-nature-related-issues-the-leap-approach/ · https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2023/10/21/tnfd-recommendations-for-nature-related-disclosures/ · https://www.lw.com/admin/upload/SiteAttachments/TNFD-Publishes-Finalized-Recommendations-for-Nature-Related-Disclosures.pdf · https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/aa42ae61/taskforce-on-nature-related-financial-disclosures-publishes-draft-sector-specific-guidance

## 3. Thematic Deep-Dives

### 3.1 Mine closure, rehabilitation & financial assurance

**Standard of care:** closure is planned from **day one** and executed **progressively** through life-of-asset, not deferred to end-of-mine. Anchor references:
- **ICMM "Integrated Mine Closure: Good Practice Guide"** — a risk-and-opportunity-based, iterative process covering *progressive*, *final*, and *sudden/temporary* closure, with clear, achievable **completion criteria** and **relinquishment**.
- Financial accounting via **IAS 37** (provisions for decommissioning/restoration obligations).
- **Financial assurance** (a.k.a. financial provision / closure / reclamation / rehabilitation bond) sized to **third-party cost** of closure, reflecting post-mining land use, stakeholder objectives and regulatory minimums — held so the public purse never funds an abandoned mine.

**World-class practice signals:** progressive-rehabilitation % against approved plans tracked annually (leading operators report ~88% progressive-closure implementation with all plans regulator-approved); consolidated closure-cost-estimate (CCE) / environmental-liability disclosed and updated; tiered rehabilitation levies; data transparency to relinquishment.

> **MD capability:** maintain a per-asset closure plan with live CCE, progressive-rehab tracker, financial-assurance instrument register and shortfall alerting; book the IAS 37 provision; align with the Tanzanian rehabilitation-bond requirement (§4.5).

Sources: https://www.icmm.com/integrated-mine-closure · https://www.goldfields.com/mine-closure.php · https://www.iisd.org/system/files/2021-09/financial-assurance-governance-for-post-mining-transition.pdf · https://www.mmg.com/sustainability/environmental-management/mine-closure-planning/

### 3.3 Water stewardship & energy

**ICMM Water Stewardship Maturity Framework** (published 2023/2024) — five elements, each with three maturity stages (**basic → advanced → leading**):
1. Governance & strategy
2. Understanding water context, risks & opportunities
3. Integration in business planning & decision-making
4. Performance & measurement
5. Transparency & reporting

Approach is **catchment-/context-based** (site *and* watershed actions), stakeholder-inclusive, and demands **context-based water targets** (a litre saved in a water-stressed catchment ≠ a litre saved in a wet one). Aligns with the **CEO Water Mandate / AWS Standard** (Alliance for Water Stewardship). **UNVERIFIED** specifics of AWS — confirm before citing.

> **MD capability:** per-site water balance (withdrawal/discharge/recycle), catchment water-stress overlay, context-based targets, and ICMM-maturity self-grading.

### 3.4 Carbon accounting & decarbonization

- **Measurement:** **GHG Protocol Corporate Standard** (Scope 1/2) + **Corporate Value Chain (Scope 3) Standard** — the universal accounting basis underpinning ISSB/CDP/CSRD/CA SB 253.
- **Targets:** **SBTi Corporate Net-Zero Standard** — near-term targets must cover **≥67%** of Scope 3; long-term **≥90%**; CNZS v2 in development (draft Apr 2025, final expected 2027) splitting Scope 1 and Scope 2 targets.
- **Industry commitment:** **ICMM members commit to net-zero Scope 1 + 2 by 2050**, accelerated decarbonisation, and **Scope 3 targets** (set ASAP). For most miners, Scope 3 (processing + use of sold minerals/fossil-feedstock) dwarfs Scope 1+2 — so Scope-3 disclosure (ISSB all-15-categories) and engagement is the material front.
- **Decarbonisation levers:** fleet electrification / trolley-assist / battery haul trucks, renewable PPAs + on-site solar/wind + storage, green-hydrogen for heat/reductants, electrified comminution, methane/fugitive controls.

Sources: https://ghgprotocol.org/ghg-protocol-corporate-suite-standards-and-guidance-update-process · https://files.sciencebasedtargets.org/production/files/Net-Zero-Standard.pdf · https://sciencebasedtargets.org/net-zero · https://www.icmm.com/en-gb/our-principles/position-statements/climate-change · https://www.icmm.com/en-gb/our-work/decarbonisation

### 3.5 Community, human rights, FPIC & ASM formalization

- **UN Guiding Principles on Business & Human Rights (UNGPs)** — protect/respect/remedy; human-rights due diligence + operational-level grievance mechanisms. (Foundational; **UNVERIFIED** in fetched set — confirm OHCHR source.)

### 3.6 Responsible-minerals / conflict-minerals due diligence (OECD)

The **OECD Due Diligence Guidance for Responsible Supply Chains of Minerals from Conflict-Affected and High-Risk Areas (CAHRAs)** is the globally-endorsed, government-backed framework. Applies to **all minerals**, all supply-chain actors, globally — and is the foundation of **EU Conflict Minerals Regulation** and US **Dodd-Frank Section 1502** (3TG: **tin, tantalum, tungsten, gold**) compliance.

**The five-step, risk-based framework:**
1. **Establish strong company management systems** — policy (the Model Supply Chain Policy), internal capacity, supplier engagement, chain-of-custody/traceability, grievance mechanism.
2. **Identify & assess risks** in the supply chain — red-flag review, map factual circumstances, prioritise.
3. **Design & implement a strategy to respond to risks** — report to senior management; **disengage only from the most severe abuses**; otherwise use leverage + risk-mitigation plans with measurable improvement.
4. **Independent third-party audit** of due diligence at identified **control points** (smelters/refiners).
5. **Publicly report** on supply-chain due-diligence policy & practice.

**Annex II risks** (the substantive red lines): (1) serious human-rights abuses (torture, forced/child labour, sexual violence, war crimes); (2) direct/indirect support to non-state armed groups; (3) abuses by public/private security forces; (4) bribery & fraudulent misrepresentation of mineral origin; (5) money-laundering & non-payment of taxes/royalties/fees to government. Supplements exist for **Tin/Tantalum/Tungsten** and for **Gold**.

> **MD capability:** maintain chain-of-custody to mine of origin, CAHRA screening, Annex-II red-flag scoring per supplier/lot, smelter/refiner audit-status register, and an annual public due-diligence report.

Sources: https://www.duediligenceguidance.org/ · https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/sub-issues/due-diligence-guidance-for-responsible-business-conduct/responsible-mineral-supply-chains.html · https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/public/doc/268/268.en.pdf · https://policy.trade.ec.europa.eu/development-and-sustainability/conflict-minerals-regulation/regulation-explained_en

## 4. Binding Law — Tanzania (Launch Jurisdiction) + East Africa

### 4.1 The 2017 reform package (the regime-defining moment)

In **July 2017**, Tanzania enacted three Acts that re-wrote the relationship between the State and investors:
1. **Natural Wealth and Resources (Permanent Sovereignty) Act, 2017** — asserts permanent sovereignty over natural resources; **disputes must be adjudicated in Tanzanian courts/bodies — international arbitration is excluded**; resources held in trust for the people.
2. **Natural Wealth and Resources Contracts (Review and Re-Negotiation of Unconscionable Terms) Act, 2017** — empowers the National Assembly to **review and order renegotiation/removal of "unconscionable" terms** in existing contracts (stabilisation clauses, dispute-forum clauses, etc.).
3. **Written Laws (Miscellaneous Amendments) Act, 2017** — amended the **Mining Act 2010**: created the **Mining Commission**, raised royalties, added the clearing fee, mandated **government free-carried interest**, local content and beneficiation.

Sources: https://www.hsfkramer.com/insights/2017-07/significant-recent-changes-to-tanzanias-mineral-law-regime · https://resourcegovernance.org/sites/default/files/documents/tanzania-new-natural-legislation-what-will-change.pdf · https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=13b41957-55c8-4d21-aae2-e25dd173f30d

### 4.2 Royalty regime (Mining Act, Cap. 123 R.E. 2019/2023)

Royalty on **gross value** ("market value of minerals as determined through valuation"):
- **6%** — **gemstones, diamonds, uranium, and metallic minerals** (gold, copper, silver, platinum) — raised from 4% (metallic) / 5% (diamond/gemstone) by the **Finance Act 2017**.
- **4%** — special rate for **gold sold to the Bank of Tanzania**. (**UNVERIFIED** exact statutory anchor — corroborated only via secondary legal commentary; confirm against the Act's schedule.)
- **3%** — other (non-gemstone/metallic) minerals such as industrial minerals; some sources cite **1% for cut/processed gems** — **verify against the current Fourth Schedule** of the Act.
- **Salt** and certain building/industrial minerals have lower/specific rates — **UNVERIFIED**; confirm in schedule.

Sources: https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=398c68a3-74b3-441f-8888-169a3ffee73a · https://www.tumemadini.go.tz/media/uploads/publications/2025/06/29/The_Mining_Act.pdf · https://www.madini.go.tz/media/CHAPTER_123_-_THE_MINING_ACT_CHAPA_FINAL.pdf · https://www.africaninfex.com/assets/Uploads/Minerals-Royalty-Forms-and-Rates.pdf

### 4.3 Clearing fee + inspection fee + clearance centres

- **1% clearing fee** on the **gross value of all minerals exported** from Tanzania, effective **1 July 2017** (Finance Act 2017). Combined headline burden therefore ~**7%** (6% royalty + 1% clearing) on exported metallic/gem minerals before income tax.
- **Inspection fee** — payable before minerals are exported; the Act establishes **clearance/clearing-house centres** (e.g., mineral trading hubs / "Madini" markets) through which minerals must pass and be valued. Exact inspection-fee percentage is set by regulation and was **not stipulated as a fixed figure in the primary Act** — **UNVERIFIED**; confirm current rate.

Sources: https://www.hsfkramer.com/insights/2017-07/significant-recent-changes-to-tanzanias-mineral-law-regime · https://resourcegovernance.org/sites/default/files/documents/tanzania-new-natural-legislation-what-will-change.pdf

### 4.4 State participation & free-carried interest

- The State is entitled to a **minimum 16% non-dilutable free-carried interest** in the share capital of a mining company holding a **Special Mining Licence / Mining Licence** (mineral category dependent), with a right to **acquire up to 50%** commensurate with the value of tax expenditures/incentives granted. This sits alongside the Local Content **16% Tanzanian-ownership** requirement (§4.6) — *distinct mechanisms, both ~16%* — an MD must model both.

Sources: https://www.hsfkramer.com/insights/2017-07/significant-recent-changes-to-tanzanias-mineral-law-regime · https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=398c68a3-74b3-441f-8888-169a3ffee73a · https://resourcegovernance.org/sites/default/files/documents/tanzania-new-natural-legislation-what-will-change.pdf

### 4.5 Environmental law & rehabilitation (EMA 2004 + NEMC + EIA/EA)

- **Environmental Management Act (EMA), 2004 (Cap. 191)** is the framework environmental statute. The **National Environmental Management Council (NEMC)** is the enforcement/review body.
- **EIA mandatory** for mining projects: governed by the **Environmental (Impact Assessment and Audit) Regulations, 2005 (amended 2018)** — a **nine-step EIA process**; ongoing/pre-existing projects must conduct an **Environmental Audit (EA)** and obtain compliance.
- **EIA/EA must be conducted by experts/firms registered & certified by NEMC** (EMA s.83; Registration of Experts Regulations 2005 / 2021).
- An **EIA Certificate** from NEMC is a precondition to operate; environmental monitoring, audits, and **rehabilitation/closure obligations with financial assurance** flow from EMA + Mining (Environmental Protection for Small Scale Mining)/closure regulations. (Exact bond mechanics — **UNVERIFIED**; confirm current mining environmental regulations.)

Sources: https://www.nemc.or.tz/uploads/publications/sw-1576228517-ema%20act2004.pdf · https://www.nemc.or.tz/uploads/publications/sw-1576238362-EIA%20PROCEDURE.pdf · https://www.nemc.or.tz/uploads/publications/sw-1696846053-Registration%20and%20Practice%20Env.%20Experts%20REGULATIONS,%202021.pdf · https://eia.nemc.or.tz/

### 4.6 Local content + Tanzanian participation

- **Mining (Local Content) Regulations, 2018 (amended 2019)** require: procurement preference for **goods produced and services rendered in Tanzania**; a **local-content plan**; **indigenous Tanzanian company** preference; employment/training/technology-transfer (Tanzanianisation) targets; and **minimum 16% equity held by an indigenous Tanzanian company / Tanzanian shareholders** for entities in the mining value chain.
- **Primary Mining Licences (PMLs)** are **reserved for Tanzanian citizens** (recent regulatory reinforcement) — central to the ASM-formalization interface.
- A **Local Content Committee** under the Mining Commission oversees compliance.

Sources: https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=f26f7200-ee53-4271-9560-2f9b64afb8d0 · https://www.tanzaniainvest.com/mining/mining-sector-revenue-2023-2024 · https://www.madini.go.tz/media/CHAPTER_123_-_THE_MINING_ACT_CHAPA_FINAL.pdf

### 4.7 Transparency — TEITI (Tanzania EITI)

- Tanzania is an **EITI implementing country**; the **Tanzania Extractive Industries (Transparency and Accountability) Act, 2015** + **Regulations 2019** provide for **full contract disclosure** and payment reconciliation, operationalised by **TEITI** (teiti.go.tz).
- **Validation:** Tanzania achieved a **moderate** overall score (**77 points**) against the 2019 EITI Standard (Nov 2023, 3rd Validation).
- **Beneficial ownership:** Companies Act amended (2020) + BO regulations (2021) to maintain a **beneficial-ownership register**; a TEITI BO roadmap targets disclosure per EITI Requirement 2.5.
- **Gap to track:** actual extractive **contract disclosure remains limited** in practice despite the legal basis — an MD operating in TZ should *exceed* the local floor and disclose proactively.

Sources: https://eiti.org/countries/tanzania · https://www.teiti.go.tz/ · https://eiti.org/board-decision/2023-45 · https://eiti.org/documents/tanzania-eiti-beneficial-ownership-roadmap · https://www.teiti.go.tz/storage/app/uploads/public/668/064/9a9/6680649a98f4c180152519.pdf
